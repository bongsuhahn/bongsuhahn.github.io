02 / RESEARCH · AREA 04

Data-Driven Modeling & Estimation

Area Overview

Research in Data-Driven Modeling & Estimation at ROCOS focuses on extracting compact and interpretable dynamical representations from measured data when the internal states of a physical system are only partially observable. Rather than evaluating a model solely by prediction accuracy, we examine whether its latent structure is supported by measurements, whether its states and parameters can be reliably estimated, and whether the resulting representation remains meaningful across operating conditions. This perspective combines data-driven learning with dynamical-system structure, observability analysis, and state estimation to construct models that are not only accurate, but also interpretable and usable for system-level reasoning.

I / ULTIMATE GOAL

The direction of our research.

To construct measurement-supported dynamical models whose internal representations can be meaningfully interpreted, reliably estimated, and transferred across changing operating conditions.

II / CORE RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Questions that guide the work.

  1. What should the data-driven model represent?

    What level of dynamical complexity is actually supported by the available measurements, and how should model order be selected accordingly?

  2. What can actually be inferred from measurements?

    Which latent states and parameters are distinguishable from the available input-output data, and which directions remain weakly supported by measurement?

  3. How should physics and data be combined?

    How can physical structure guide learning without forcing an unjustified one-to-one correspondence between learned latent coordinates and specific physical states?

  4. How should a learned model adapt when operating conditions change?

    Which parts of the learned dynamics should remain fixed, and which low-dimensional components should adapt when the system departs from its nominal operating regime?

  5. How reliable is the model outside the training domain?

    Do predictive accuracy, latent-state interpretation, and uncertainty calibration remain reliable under protocol, operating-condition, or dataset shift?

III / METHODOLOGICAL APPROACH

From measurements to interpretable inference.

Our approach begins with measured input-output behavior and constructs a dynamical representation whose complexity is constrained by both predictive fidelity and measurement support. Candidate state representations are examined through observability, sensitivity, state distinguishability, and estimation performance, allowing weakly supported directions to be identified and model structure to be refined. State and parameter estimators are then used to recover latent information from measurements, while low-dimensional adaptive mechanisms are introduced when residual dynamics vary across operating conditions. Finally, the resulting models are evaluated not only in-domain but also across protocols, datasets, and operating regimes to distinguish predictive accuracy from genuine model transferability and uncertainty reliability.
