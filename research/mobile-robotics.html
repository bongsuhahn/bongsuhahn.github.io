02 / RESEARCH · AREA 02

Mobile Robotics

Area Overview

Exploring mobile robotic systems through sensing, motion modeling, and control, with a focus on robust behavior in changing environments and physical interactions.

Sensing · Motion Modeling · Motion Control

Sense → Estimate Motion → Control

I / ULTIMATE GOAL

The direction of our research.

To establish a reliable motion layer in which mobile robots can estimate their physical state and execute predictable motion despite limited sensing, disturbances, slip, and nonideal interaction with the environment.

II / CORE RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Questions that guide the work.

  1. How should robot motion be represented?

    What level of kinematic and dynamic detail is required to describe the actual motion of a mobile robot without making the model unnecessarily complex?

  2. What can the robot infer from onboard sensing?

    How can robot velocity, motion state, slip, and disturbances be reconstructed when direct measurement is incomplete or unavailable?

  3. How should motion control respond to physical mismatch?

    How should motion control compensate when wheel–ground interaction, disturbances, actuator characteristics, or modeling errors cause the actual robot motion to deviate from the commanded motion?

  4. How can mobility remain reliable across operating conditions?

    How can motion models and controllers remain reliable across changes in speed, maneuver, surface condition, payload, or physical interaction?

III / METHODOLOGICAL APPROACH

From sensing to robust motion.

Our approach begins with a motion representation tied to the actual geometry, actuation limits, and sensing configuration of the robot. Onboard measurements are then used to reconstruct motion states and identify deviations caused by slip, disturbances, or model mismatch. Estimation and compensation mechanisms are integrated with motion control so that the commanded motion remains physically realizable under nonideal conditions. The resulting methods are evaluated across maneuvers and operating conditions, with emphasis on the agreement between estimated motion, commanded motion, and the behavior of the physical robot.
