02 / RESEARCH · AREA 01

Control Systems

Area Overview

Control research at ROCOS focuses on how physical models, estimated states, and system information can be translated into reliable control actions when sensing is limited and the system is affected by uncertainty, disturbances, or model mismatch. Rather than treating control as an isolated controller-design problem, we consider the full connection between system dynamics, available measurements, estimation, and closed-loop behavior. This perspective supports control strategies that remain interpretable, robust, and experimentally meaningful in real engineering systems.

I / ULTIMATE GOAL

The direction of our research.

To establish reliable control methodologies that use physically meaningful models and estimated system information to maintain desired behavior under sensing limitations, disturbances, uncertainty, and changing operating conditions.

II / CORE RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Questions that guide the work.

  1. How should system dynamics be represented for control?

    Which aspects of the system dynamics must be retained so that the model remains useful for control without introducing unnecessary complexity?

  2. How can unmeasured quantities support control?

    How can states, forces, torques, disturbances, or other inaccessible physical quantities be estimated with sufficient reliability to support feedback and control decisions?

  3. How should control respond to uncertainty and model mismatch?

    How can control strategies remain reliable when the actual system deviates from its nominal model because of disturbances, parameter variations, unmodeled dynamics, or changing operating conditions?

  4. How should model-based and data-informed control be combined?

    How can physical models and information inferred from data be integrated so that each contributes where it is most reliable, without losing interpretability or system-level consistency?

III / METHODOLOGICAL APPROACH

From system dynamics to closed-loop validation.

Our approach begins with a control-oriented representation of the system dynamics and identifies the quantities that must be measured or estimated for closed-loop operation. State, disturbance, force, or torque estimators are introduced when direct sensing is unavailable or impractical. Control laws are then formulated to account explicitly for uncertainty, disturbances, parameter variation, and residual model mismatch. When necessary, adaptive mechanisms or data-informed corrections are incorporated without discarding the physical structure of the system. The resulting methods are evaluated through closed-loop performance and experimental validation rather than model accuracy alone.
